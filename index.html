Hi, I'm Zijie Jin 金子捷.

PhD Student in Electrical & Computer Engineering

I am a researcher at the University of Washington, advised by Dr. Kimberly Ingraham. My work operates at the intersection of engineering and human health, utilizing game theory paradigms to investigate how humans interact and co-adapt with assistive wearable devices.

My background spans Chemical, Biological, and Biomedical Engineering. Previously, I worked on microbiome bioreactors at McGill University and exoskeleton dynamics at Polytechnique Montréal. I am driven by the design and control of systems that augment human capability.

Research Experience

Current • Ingraham Lab (UW)

Human-Exoskeleton Co-adaptation

Investigating how humans interact with assistive devices in the context of locomotion biomechanics.

  • Modeling interactions using a game theory paradigm.
  • Developing adaptive control schemes for lower-limb exoskeletons to minimize metabolic cost.
  • Quantifying human adaptation to novel split-belt walking conditions.
2022-2023 • CoSIM (Polytechnique Montréal)

Exoskeleton Dynamics & Control

Research Assistant focusing on biomechanics and sliding mode controllers.

  • Modeled dynamics of human lower limbs wearing 1-DOF knee and 3-DOF lower limb exoskeletons.
  • Simulated control strategies implementing non-linear disturbance observers for joint torque.
2019-2022 • Micro & Nano Lab (McGill)

Mini Colon Model (MiCoMo)

Master's thesis project developing in vitro artificial colon simulators.

  • Developed a low-cost, high-throughput bioreactor system mimicking the GI microbial environment.
  • Programmed microcontrollers for automatic control of anaerobic conditions, pH, and temperature.
  • Validated device efficacy via 16S rRNA sequencing.
